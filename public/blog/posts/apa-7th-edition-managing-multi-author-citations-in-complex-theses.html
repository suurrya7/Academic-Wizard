What is the APA 7th Edition Rule for Three or More Authors?

Under the APA 7th edition guidelines, the rules for multi-author citations have been simplified to reduce word counts and improve readability. For any work with three or more authors, you should use the "et al." abbreviation from the very first in-text citation.

This is a significant shift from previous editions, which required listing all authors upon the first mention. To ensure your APA 7 in-text citation guidelines are followed to the letter, remember these three core principles:

  • Consistency: Even if you mention the same source multiple times, always use the surname of the first author followed by "et al."
  • Punctuation: "Et al." is a Latin abbreviation meaning "and others." Always place a full stop after "al" and ensure there is a space before it if it is parenthetical.
  • Clarity: If multiple sources share the same first author and publication year, you must add enough author names to distinguish between the two entries.

How to Manage Complex Multi-Author Citations in Your Thesis

Citing academic sources APA 7 across a 15,000 to 50,000-word thesis requires a systematic workflow. When your document involves dozens of references, even minor formatting slips can compound. Using a free citation generator can help you verify your bibliography, but your in-text citations remain your responsibility to check.

To maintain precision, follow this structure for your in-text entries:

  1. Parenthetical Citation: (Smith et al., 2023)
  2. Narrative Citation: Smith et al. (2023) argued that...
  3. Handling Ambiguity: If you have two sources—(Smith, Jones, & Brown, 2022) and (Smith, Williams, & Davis, 2022)—you must list as many names as needed to distinguish the citations.

Common Mistakes When Formatting Graduate Thesis References

Formatting errors often occur during the transition from rough draft to final submission. When handling et al in APA, students frequently forget that "et al." should never be italicised in the body of the text. Furthermore, the year must always follow the abbreviation without any extraneous punctuation other than the comma.

Another common mistake is the misuse of the ampersand (&). In narrative citations (where the author's name is part of your sentence), you must use the word "and." In parenthetical citations (placed at the end of a sentence in brackets), you use the ampersand symbol.

If your thesis involves a blend of digital media, journals, and books, you must be careful not to conflate rules. Always refer back to your specific source type to ensure your formatting remains compliant with APA 7 standards.

Best Practices for Maintaining Academic Tone

Beyond the technical rules, your citations should support your arguments, not interrupt them. A well-cited thesis demonstrates that you have engaged with the relevant literature in your field. When managing your references, keep the following ethical considerations in mind:

  • Accuracy: Never rely on secondary citations if you can access the primary source.
  • Voice: Ensure your citations are integrated smoothly so that your unique academic voice remains the primary focus of the thesis.
  • Verification: Proofread your reference list against your in-text citations at least twice before final submission.
