Why Digital Media Citations Are Changing

In the digital age, source material has evolved beyond standard journal articles and books. We now regularly cite social media posts, webinars, podcasts, and even interactive datasets. APA 7th edition has simplified these requirements by focusing on the "who, when, what, and where" of a source, rather than forcing every medium into a rigid template. How to Cite Complex Digital Media

Citing non-traditional sources requires careful attention to the "where" and "what." If a source is dynamic—meaning the content changes over time—you should include a retrieval date. For static digital content, this is unnecessary. Here are the rules for common digital hurdles:

  • Social Media: Use the account name as the author, followed by the handle in brackets. Include the specific date of the post.
  • Webinars and Videos: Identify the creator, the date, the title (in italics), the format in brackets, and the site name or URL.
  • Podcasts: If citing a single episode, list the host as the author and the episode number in the title section.

What Are the Rules for Multi-Author Citations?

One of the most common areas where students lose marks is the inconsistent handling of multi-author sources. APA 7th edition introduced streamlined rules for in-text citations that help reduce word count while maintaining clarity.

For works with three or more authors, you should use the first author’s surname followed by "et al." from the very first citation. This differs significantly from older editions where you had to list every name once. APA 7 Reference List Tips for Success

Your bibliography is the final impression you leave on your markers. A clean, well-formatted list demonstrates attention to detail. Follow these golden rules for your reference list:

  • Alphabetical Order: References must be ordered alphabetically by the first author's surname.
  • Hanging Indents: Every entry must use a hanging indent, where the first line is flush left and subsequent lines are indented.
  • Double-Spacing: The entire list should be double-spaced without extra lines between entries.
  • URL Integrity: Ensure URLs are live and hyperlinks are active unless you are submitting a physical print copy.

How to Handle Missing Information in Digital Sources

Modern digital media occasionally lacks standard elements like an author or a publication date. APA 7 provides specific placeholders for these instances:

  • No Author: Move the title of the work to the author position.
  • No Date: Use "(n.d.)" in place of the year.
  • No Page Numbers: If citing an online source without pagination, use the paragraph number (e.g., para. 4) or the section heading.

Consistency is your best defence. If you cannot find a date, ensure you cite the same way throughout the document.
