What is the core philosophy of APA 7 for multiple authors?

The American Psychological Association (APA) simplified its approach in the 7th edition to prioritize clarity and reduce wordiness. The primary objective is to make your academic formatting for long papers consistent and easy for the reader to track. Unlike previous iterations, the 7th edition focuses on immediately identifying the primary author while shortening the list of contributors for works with three or more authors.

How to format in-text citations for multiple authors

The rules for in-text citations depend on the number of authors and whether you are using a parenthetical or narrative citation. Following this free citation generator can help you automate the process, but understanding the underlying logic is key to manual verification.

  • One author: Use the author’s surname and the year (e.g., Smith, 2024).
  • Two authors: Always cite both names every time they appear (e.g., Smith & Jones, 2024).
  • Three or more authors: Use the surname of the first author followed by "et al." starting with the first citation (e.g., Smith et al., 2024).

Note that "et al." is Latin for "and others." Always ensure there is a period after "al" and a comma if it precedes a date in a parenthetical citation. Precision here is a hallmark of professional academic writing.

How to handle reference list formatting for 2026

The reference list is where your complex citation management becomes most visible. While in-text citations are abbreviated for three or more authors, the reference list requires a more detailed approach:

  • Works with up to 20 authors: List all authors by name.
  • Works with 21 or more authors: List the first 19 authors, insert an ellipsis (...), and then add the name of the final author.
  • Ordering: Ensure your reference list is alphabetized by the first author's surname.
  • Hanging Indent: Use a hanging indent for every entry, where the first line is flush left and subsequent lines are indented.

Common pitfalls in long-form Canadian theses

One common mistake is failing to differentiate between narrative and parenthetical citations. In a narrative citation, you integrate the authors' names into the flow of your sentence. In a parenthetical citation, the authors and the year are placed inside brackets at the end of the sentence.

Furthermore, ensure you are not using "&" inside your sentences. Always use "and" in narrative text, reserving the ampersand ("&") strictly for parenthetical citations. Consistency is the primary marker of a student who has mastered APA 7 in-text citation guide principles.
