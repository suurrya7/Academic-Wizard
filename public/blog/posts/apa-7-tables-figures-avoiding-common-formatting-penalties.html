What is the APA 7 Standard for Tables?

Tables should present data in a concise, readable format. According to the APA 7th edition, a table must include the following components, ordered from top to bottom:

  • Table Number: Bolded, appearing above the table title (e.g., Table 1).
  • Table Title: A brief, descriptive title in title case and italics, placed on the line below the table number.
  • The Body: The data itself, ensuring headers are clear and horizontal lines are used sparingly.
  • Note: A brief explanation below the table, starting with the word "Note." italicised.

Avoid vertical lines. APA style explicitly prefers an open, clean look where only essential horizontal borders are used to separate the header row from the data. How to Correctly Format Figures in APA 7

Figures encompass everything that is not a table, including charts, diagrams, maps, and photographs. The layout for figures differs slightly from tables:

  • Figure Number: Bolded, placed above the image.
  • Figure Title: Descriptive, italicised, and placed one double-spaced line below the figure number.
  • The Image: The visual aid itself, centred within the margins.
  • Figure Legend/Note: Placed within the figure itself or directly below it, clearly explaining the visual components.

Ensure that all labels within your figure are of a readable, sans-serif font like Calibri or Arial, even if your main body text is a serif font. This improves digital accessibility and demonstrates that you understand the nuances of thesis visual aids.

Avoiding Common Compliance Traps

One common mistake is failing to mention a table or figure in the text. You must refer to every visual element by its number (e.g., "As shown in Table 2...") before it appears in the document. Do not use phrases like "the table above" or "the figure below," as page breaks can shift your layout during the final submission process.

Another major trap is the improper use of copyright attribution. If you have adapted a table or figure from another source, you must include a citation in the note beneath the figure. Failing to do this can trigger integrity concerns.
