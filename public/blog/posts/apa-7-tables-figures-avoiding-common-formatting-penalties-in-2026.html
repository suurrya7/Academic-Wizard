What is the Purpose of Tables and Figures in APA 7?

The primary role of tables and figures is to supplement, not duplicate, your text. They should provide a visual summary that allows your reader to grasp complex patterns quickly. If a table contains only two or three rows, consider describing it in the text instead. How to Master APA 7th Edition Table Formatting Rules

Formatting tables is a common academic pitfall. To ensure your submission is professional, follow these strict guidelines:

  • Table Number: Use bold, Arabic numerals (e.g., Table 1) placed above the table.
  • Table Title: Place the title one double-spaced line below the table number in italicised, title case.
  • Headings: Use only horizontal lines. APA 7 specifically forbids vertical lines within the table.
  • Spacings: Tables should be double-spaced. If a table is dense, single-spacing or 1.5-spacing is permissible for readability, provided it remains consistent.
  • Notes: Any explanations, abbreviations, or source citations must be placed in a "Note" section directly below the table.

APA 7 Figure Caption Standards

Figures—which include graphs, charts, photographs, and drawings—carry their own specific requirements. Unlike tables, which have the title above, figure information is placed directly below the image:

  1. Figure Number: Bold, Arabic numeral (e.g., Figure 1).
  2. Caption: Descriptive title below the figure number.
  3. Body: The actual image or illustration.
  4. Note: Any source attribution or legend explanation goes after the caption.

Always ensure that the resolution of your images is high enough for print or digital submission. Blurry or pixelated charts are a significant mark-down factor in UK universities.

Common Academic Formatting Pitfalls to Avoid

Even the most diligent students fall into traps that can be easily avoided. These are the most frequent issues our editors encounter:

  • Duplication: Do not repeat the data from a table in your main body text. Use the text to highlight key trends or interesting outliers instead.
  • Inconsistent Font Sizes: Keep the font within your tables and figures the same size as your main document (typically 12pt Times New Roman or 11pt Calibri).
  • Missing Citations: If you did not create the table or figure yourself, you must cite the source in the note section, even if you have adapted the data.
  • Alignment Errors: Ensure all decimal points are vertically aligned, as this aids in data comparison.

Ensuring Citation Style Compliance

Beyond visual formatting, the accuracy of your source attribution within tables and figures is paramount. In the UK, academic integrity is strictly monitored. If your table contains information from an external source, the citation must follow the standard APA 7 in-text format. Ensure you have properly cross-referenced your Bibliography with your table notes.
