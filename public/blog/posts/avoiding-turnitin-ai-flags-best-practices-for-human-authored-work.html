Turnitin’s AI writing detection tool is designed to identify patterns in writing that are characteristic of Large Language Models (LLMs). These models typically generate text with a high degree of "burstiness" and "perplexity." While this sounds sophisticated, it essentially means the AI predicts the most likely next word in a sequence.

The system evaluates the likelihood that a text was generated by AI based on its database of model-written samples. However, Turnitin AI detector accuracy is not infallible. High-quality academic writing often employs predictable structures, standard terminology, and formulaic transitions, which can unfortunately mimic the statistical patterns identified by detection algorithms.

Proving Original Academic Authorship

The best way to prove your authorship is to maintain a robust "paper trail" throughout your research process. In the UK university system, academic integrity is paramount. If a department questions your similarity score, you should be able to produce the following:

  1. Drafting History: Keep dated versions of your document in Microsoft Word or Google Docs. Showing the evolution of an argument over weeks or months is compelling evidence of human labour.
  2. Research Notes: Keep organised logs of your reading and initial thoughts.
  3. Annotated Bibliography: Demonstrate how you engaged with each source beyond a simple summary.
