What is AI-Assisted Scaffolding in Research?

AI scaffolding refers to the strategic use of machine learning tools to organize research, identify thematic clusters, and suggest logical hierarchies for your dissertation. Unlike generative writing, which may produce synthetic prose that violates institutional integrity policies, scaffolding focuses on taxonomy and flow. It acts as a digital research assistant that keeps your findings organized.

When used correctly, these tools assist in:

  • Mapping connections between disparate studies.
  • Categorizing themes to support a dissertation synthesis framework.
  • Identifying gaps in the current academic discourse.
  • Visualizing the evolution of specific theoretical constructs over time.

How to Organize Your Literature Review Structure Ethically

The goal is to maintain human-led inquiry while leveraging computational speed. Follow these steps to build a robust, ethical outline:

1. Data Preparation and Categorization

Before prompting any AI, perform a manual thematic audit. Group your sources by methodology, theoretical lens, or chronological progression. If you are handling complex qualitative data, you may find that NVivo 26: Building a Reliable Coding Frame for Qualitative Data provides the necessary rigour to keep your themes distinct and bias-free.

2. Ethical Prompt Engineering

When using AI to outline, your prompts should focus on classification rather than creation. Use the following structure to maintain ethical standards:

  • Input: Provide a list of titles and abstracts from your own Zotero library.
  • Constraint: Instruct the AI to "Sort these sources into three distinct thematic clusters based on their primary methodology."
  • Verification: Always cross-reference the AI’s categorization against your own reading of the texts to ensure accuracy.

3. Implementing a Synthesis Framework

An effective literature review structure should move beyond a "book report" style. Use your AI-generated skeleton to create a thematic argument. Each section of your outline should reflect a specific scholarly debate, not just a list of authors.
