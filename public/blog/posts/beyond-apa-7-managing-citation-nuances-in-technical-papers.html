How to Manage Complex In-Text Citations

One of the most frequent hurdles in technical papers is citing multiple works by the same author in the same year or dealing with corporate authors. To streamline your workflow and avoid common pitfalls, review our detailed guide on APA 7th Edition: Navigating Complex Multi-Author In-Text Citations.

When you encounter tricky sources, keep these three rules in mind to maintain your flow:

  • Three or more authors: Always use the first author’s name followed by "et al." starting from the very first citation. Do not list every name in the text.
  • Multiple sources in one parenthesis: Order them alphabetically by the first author's name, separated by semicolons.
  • Direct quotations: You must include the page or paragraph number. For technical papers, if page numbers are absent, use the section heading or paragraph number for clarity.

What is the Correct Way to Handle Corporate Authors?

Technical reports often rely on data from government agencies or large organizations. In APA 7, the rule is to spell out the full name of the group author unless the abbreviation is well-known. If an abbreviation is used, define it upon the first citation.

For example, if you are citing Statistics Canada:

  • First citation: (Statistics Canada [StatCan], 2024)
  • Subsequent citations: (StatCan, 2024)

By following this APA style reference guide, you prevent confusion and make it easier for your reader to track your sources back to their origin.

How to Troubleshoot Your Reference List

A common error in technical papers is the disconnect between the in-text citation and the reference list. Every cited source must appear in your list, and every list entry must be cited. Use this checklist to troubleshoot your final pages:

  1. Alphabetical Order: Ensure your reference list is sorted by the first author’s surname.
  2. Hanging Indents: Every entry must use a hanging indent (the first line is flush left, subsequent lines are indented).
  3. DOI/URL formatting: APA 7 requires that DOIs and URLs are presented as hyperlinks. Ensure they are functional and properly formatted.
  4. Italics: Titles of reports, books, and journal names must be italicized, but article or chapter titles should not be.

Refining Your Writing for Canadian Academic Standards

Canadian academic writing often requires specific attention to spelling conventions (e.g., using "colour" instead of "color" or "centre" instead of "center"). While APA style is American-based, your institutional guidelines likely require Canadian spelling throughout the body of your paper, including your references when referring to Canadian organizations.
