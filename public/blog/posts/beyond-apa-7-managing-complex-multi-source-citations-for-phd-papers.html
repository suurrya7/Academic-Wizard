How to Manage Multi-Author In-Text Citation APA 7

The most common area where students falter is in the handling of long author lists. Under APA 7th edition complex citation rules, the "et al." rule has been simplified, yet many students continue to use archaic, outdated methods.

  • One or two authors: Always cite both names every time.
  • Three or more authors: Use the first author’s name followed by "et al." from the very first citation.
  • Multiple works in one bracket: Order them alphabetically by the first author's name, separated by semicolons.

How to Handle Secondary Data and Archival Sources

PhD theses often incorporate secondary analysis. Citing these correctly is a nuanced task. If you are secondary-analysing a dataset, you must cite both the original author and the data repository. Failing to credit the repository can lead to claims of poor source management.

Here are the steps to follow for complex archival or data sources:

  1. Identify if the source has a DOI or a stable URL.
  2. Determine if the author is an individual or an organisation (corporate author).
  3. Use the "Retrieved from" prefix only if a retrieval date is necessary (e.g., for wikis or dynamic databases).
  4. Ensure the reference entry includes the specific format (e.g., [Data set], [Audio podcast]) in square brackets.

Best Practices for Postgraduate Research Referencing

To avoid the stress of manual fixes, integrate these habits into your daily workflow:

  • Use Reference Management Software: Tools like Zotero or Mendeley are essential for keeping large bibliographies organised.
  • Verify Automatically Generated Citations: Never trust a tool blindly. AI and automated systems occasionally misinterpret author names or publication dates.
  • Perform a Final Audit: Conduct a "control-find" search for "et al." to ensure they are all formatted correctly and consistently throughout the document.
  • Check for Missing DOI Links: In the era of digital publishing, examiners expect functional hyperlinks in your bibliography.
