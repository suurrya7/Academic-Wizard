What is Researcher Bias in Qualitative Theses?

Researcher bias occurs when your pre-existing beliefs, expectations, or theoretical commitments influence how you collect, code, and analyse your data. In a PhD context, this can lead to "cherry-picking" quotes that support a desired hypothesis or ignoring "outlier" data that challenges your emerging narrative.

While complete objectivity is rarely possible, you can achieve "reflexivity"—the process of being acutely aware of your own influence. How to Avoid Coding Drift in Your Thesis

Coding drift happens when your interpretation of a code changes as you progress through your data. If you code your first 10 interviews differently than your last 10, your findings will be skewed. To maintain consistency:

  • Review Initial Nodes: Re-code two or three early interviews halfway through your project to see if your interpretation has shifted.
  • Consolidate and Refine: Use the "Merge" feature in NVivo if you find that you have created redundant nodes that actually represent the same concept.
  • Check for "Data Saturation": Use the chart tools in NVivo to see if new interviews are still providing novel codes or if your themes have stabilised.
