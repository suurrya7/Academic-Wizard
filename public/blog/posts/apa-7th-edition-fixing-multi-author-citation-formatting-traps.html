What is the core logic behind APA 7th edition multi-author in-text citation rules?

The APA 7th edition simplified many of its guidelines to make research more readable. The fundamental rule for in-text citations revolves around the number of authors identified in your source. Whether you are using a narrative citation or a parenthetical one, the following rules apply:

  • One Author: Use the author’s last name (e.g., Smith, 2024).
  • Two Authors: Always cite both names every time they appear (e.g., Smith & Jones, 2024).
  • Three or More Authors: Use the first author’s last name followed by "et al." from the very first citation (e.g., Smith et al., 2024).

Note that "et al." is Latin for "and others." It is essential to include the period after "al" but not after "et." Getting these small details right is a hallmark of strong academic citation formatting.

How to handle complex cases in your bibliography

While in-text rules are straightforward, your reference list demands more precision. The apa 7 citation guide dictates that for a source with up to 20 authors, you must list all of them in the bibliography. If there are 21 or more authors, you list the first 19, insert an ellipsis (...), and then list the final author.

Fixing common multi-author traps

Many students encounter issues when multiple sources result in the same "et al." citation. If you have two different sources that shorten to the same format, you must add more names until the citations are distinct. This is a common trap that requires careful attention to detail during the final proofreading stage.

Another frequent error involves the misuse of the ampersand (&). In parenthetical citations, use the ampersand between names. In narrative citations—where you mention the authors as part of the sentence—use the word "and" instead. For example: "According to Smith and Jones (2024)..." versus "(Smith & Jones, 2024)."
