What is the APA 7 standard for multi-author citations?

The APA 7th Edition simplified many citation conventions to improve readability and consistency. For sources with three or more authors, the rules are now uniform across both parenthetical and narrative citations. You no longer need to list every single author the first time a source is mentioned.

Instead, you use the "et al." abbreviation from the very first mention. The term "et al." is Latin for "and others." It is essential to ensure you punctuate it correctly: there is a full stop after "al" because it is an abbreviation, but there is no full stop after "et."

The primary rule: Three or more authors

Regardless of whether you are writing for a UK-based institution or an international journal, the rule for sources with three or more authors remains constant:

  • First author's surname + et al. + year of publication.

For example, if you are citing a study by Harrison, Miller, Taylor, and Davies (2024), your in-text citation would simply appear as (Harrison et al., 2024). This streamlines your writing, allowing the reader to focus on your synthesis of ideas rather than a cluttered list of names.

Narrative vs. Parenthetical

In a narrative citation, you integrate the authors into the flow of your sentence:

Example: Harrison et al. (2024) argued that empirical data is essential for qualitative rigour.

In a parenthetical citation, the source details appear inside brackets at the end of the sentence or clause:

Example: Qualitative data requires specific handling to maintain research integrity (Harrison et al., 2024).

APA 7 reference list: The full picture

The beauty of the APA 7 multi-author in-text citation rules is their brevity, but your reference list must provide the complete information. The APA 7 reference list requirements dictate that for works with up to 20 authors, you must include every name. It is only when a work has 21 or more authors that you truncate the list with an ellipsis after the 19th author.

Best practices for citing academic papers

When you are deep in the writing process, it is easy to lose track of citation placement. Keep these three tips in mind to remain consistent:

  1. Check author count carefully: Ensure you have counted the authors correctly before applying the "et al." rule.
  2. Punctuation placement: The comma after "et al." is only required in specific sentence structures; usually, it is just "et al." followed by the year.
  3. Consistency is key: Ensure that your choice of narrative or parenthetical citation serves the flow of your argument.

Common mistakes to avoid

Even experienced academics occasionally misapply the rules. Avoid these frequent errors to keep your work professional:

  • Italicising "et al.": Do not put "et al." in italics. It is a standard abbreviation, not a foreign term in this context.
  • Adding "and": Avoid writing "and et al." since "et" already means "and." It is redundant.
  • Misplacing punctuation: Always place the full stop after the closing parenthesis, not inside it, unless the citation is a block quote.
