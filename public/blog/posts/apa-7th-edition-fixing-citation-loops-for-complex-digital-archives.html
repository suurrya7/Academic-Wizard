What is the APA 7th Edition approach to digital archives?

The APA 7th edition emphasises that the goal of a reference is to allow a reader to relocate the source. When dealing with archives, the challenge lies in the "who, what, when, and where." Many students fall into the trap of using a standard webpage template for a primary source document, which often results in lost metadata.

To master complex APA 7 referencing, you must identify the four core components of a digital archive reference:

  • Author: Who created the document, letter, or photograph? If it is an organisation, treat them as the author.
  • Date: Use the specific date of the document if available. If it is undated, use "n.d."
  • Title: The name of the item. If the item has no formal title, provide a brief description in square brackets.
  • Archive Details: The name of the archive and the URL or DOI.

How to handle non-traditional media in digital collections

Citing non-traditional media requires a flexible approach. Sometimes, you are not just citing a document; you are citing a digital copy of a physical object housed in a specific collection. If the archive uses a specific identification number, always include it in your reference.

Consider this structure for a document from a digital collection:

Author, A. A. (Year, Month Day). Title of document [Description of item]. Name of Collection (Collection number). Name of Archive. URL

Best practices for digital source attribution

Digital source attribution is not just about avoiding plagiarism; it is about providing a roadmap for future researchers. In Australia, markers look for precision. If your digital source is highly volatile—meaning it may disappear or change—make sure you keep a local copy or a PDF capture in your project files.

Follow these APA 7 formatting tips to keep your reference list clean:

  • Be specific with brackets: If the media is an audio file, a video, or an unpublished letter, always include the format in square brackets after the title.
  • Prioritise the archive name: Unlike standard webpages, digital archives (like the National Archives of Australia) should be clearly identified as the publisher.
  • Use the URL as the final element: If a persistent identifier like a DOI is available, prioritise it over a standard URL.

Overcoming common hurdles in APA 7 digital referencing

One of the biggest issues students face is "citation loops," where one source leads to another, and you are unsure which one to cite. Always cite the item you have actually viewed. If you are viewing a digital scan of a 19th-century manuscript, cite the manuscript as the primary source, including the name of the digital archive where you accessed it.
