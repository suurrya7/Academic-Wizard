How to Format Citations for Multiple Authors

The transition from older styles to APA 7 has been a relief for many, but the "three or more" rule requires close attention. Here is how you handle authorship in your citations:

  • One author: Use the author's surname.
  • Two authors: Use both surnames, separated by an ampersand (&) in parentheses, or "and" in the narrative.
  • Three or more authors: Always use the first author’s surname followed by "et al." from the very first citation.

When dealing with digital media, such as a podcast with multiple hosts, cite the hosts as authors. If the creators are credited as "producers" or "executives," treat them as the primary attribution point in your reference list. Remember that digital sources often require a URL or DOI. If the source is unstable or likely to change, ensure you capture the archived version or provide the most permanent link available.

Handling Digital Source Attribution with Precision

Digital sources often obscure authorship. You might find a social media thread or a multi-author blog post where personal names are absent, substituted instead by usernames or organizational handles. In such cases, APA 7 reference formatting dictates that you prioritize the entity responsible for the content.

Best Practices for Digital Media Citations

  1. Identify the primary contributors: If individuals are listed, use their names. If only a company or collective handle is available, treat that as the corporate author.
  2. Date precision: Digital media evolves. Always include the full date (Year, Month Day) if available to help your readers identify the specific version of the content you accessed.
  3. Include the platform name: Mentioning the platform (e.g., YouTube, Spotify, LinkedIn) as the site name is crucial for helping others locate the source.
  4. Check for updates: If the digital source has been updated, indicate the most recent date of access or publication to maintain academic currency.
