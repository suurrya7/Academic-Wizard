What is the APA 7th Edition Rule for Multi-Author Citations?

Ensure you reference authoritative sources. For further reading, consult APA Style Guidelines.

The APA 7th edition simplified many guidelines, but it introduced specific thresholds for how many names to include. Understanding these thresholds is essential for maintaining your academic integrity and ensuring your paper meets standard university requirements.

Here are the foundational rules for parenthetical and narrative citations:

  • One or two authors: Always include the names of all authors in every citation.
  • Three or more authors: Use the surname of the first author followed by "et al." starting with the very first citation in your text.

While this seems straightforward, students often slip up by including too many names in their in-text citations or failing to format the reference list correctly. How to Handle Complex Reference List Formatting

The reference list has different requirements than in-text citations. While you use "et al." in the body of your paper, your reference list entry must be comprehensive. This is one of the most common APA 7 formatting mistakes that editors frequently flag.

For sources with multiple authors, adhere to these guidelines:

  • 1 to 20 authors: List all authors by name. Provide the surname and initials for every author in the list.
  • 21 or more authors: List the first 19 authors, then insert an ellipsis (...), and add the final author’s name. Do not use an ampersand (&) before the final name in this specific case.

Keeping track of these details is vital for citing papers with many authors, especially in scientific fields where collaborative research is the norm. Common Pitfalls in Academic Formatting

Even seasoned researchers fall victim to technical formatting traps. Beyond the multi-author rules, many students struggle with consistency across their entire document. Proper academic writing formatting help goes beyond just names and dates; it involves correct punctuation, indentation, and hanging indents for your references.

Avoid these frequent errors:

  • Inconsistent punctuation: Ensure you use commas appropriately between author names.
  • Misplacing the ampersand: Remember that the ampersand is only used in parenthetical citations and the reference list, never in a narrative (sentence-based) citation.
  • Incorrect et al. usage: Never use "et al." for a source with only two authors.
