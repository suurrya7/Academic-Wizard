What is the core principle of citing datasets in APA 7th Edition?

The APA 7th edition approach to data is fundamentally different from citing a journal article. Because datasets are distinct, standalone entities, they require a clear author, a date, and a specific title that describes the data. When citing data in the text, you must focus on the primary contributor. If no individual author is listed, the organization or the entity that curated the data serves as the author. Remember, clarity is your priority when presenting research findings to your supervisor.

How to handle complex parenthetical citation examples

When your research involves specific statistical figures or primary datasets, you need to be precise. Here is how you should structure your apa 7 parenthetical citation examples for data:

  • Data by an individual: (Smith, 2024).
  • Data by an organization: (Statistics Canada, 2025).
  • Data with a specific retrieval location: (Global Health Data Exchange, 2023, Section 4.2).

If you are quoting or referencing a specific table within a dataset, ensure you include the specific reference point. This level of detail elevates your work and demonstrates a high degree of academic formatting for thesis writing.

Step-by-step: Citing research datasets in APA

Following the apa 7 citation guide 2026 standards requires a disciplined approach. Follow these steps to ensure you are meeting the mark:

  1. Identify the primary curator or lead researcher of the dataset.
  2. Determine the publication year or the year of the most recent version update.
  3. Use the full, descriptive title of the dataset in your reference list, but use the shortened version or organization name in the text.
  4. Always provide a DOI or URL if the data is publicly available online.

Best practices for statistical figures and tables

Tables and figures extracted from research datasets require a specific "Note" field beneath the graphic. In your text, you should refer to the table by its number, but the APA rules state you must also acknowledge the source. If the table is adapted, use the word "Adapted" followed by the citation. This shows the reader that you have synthesized the data rather than merely copying it.
