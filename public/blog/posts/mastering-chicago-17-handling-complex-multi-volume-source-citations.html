What is a multi-volume source in Chicago 17?

A multi-volume source consists of several books published under the same title, often spanning years. In Chicago style, you must distinguish between citing the entire work and citing a specific volume. Your footnote needs to lead the reader directly to the page, volume, and section of the text you have utilized.

How to format multi-volume footnotes

When you refer to a specific volume within a footnote, use the volume number followed by a comma, then the page number. Chicago 17 prefers the abbreviation "vol." in footnotes, though it is often omitted in the bibliography depending on whether the volume has a unique title.

  • Footnote structure: Author Name, Title of Work, vol. 2, Subtitle (City: Publisher, Year), 145–47.
  • Bibliography structure: Author Name. Title of Work. Vol. 2, Subtitle. City: Publisher, Year.

Handling complex archival research documentation

Archival research presents unique challenges. When working with primary source collections, the rules shift. You are often citing a document within a series, within a box, within a folder. Chicago 17 requires you to move from the smallest unit to the largest.

For instance, start with the document title, then the collection name, the box number, and finally the archival repository. This hierarchical approach ensures your archival research documentation is traceable. Without this structure, your peer reviewers or thesis committee will struggle to verify your claims.

Common pitfalls in history paper citation tips

Students often make mistakes by failing to italicize volume titles or incorrectly placing the volume number. Remember these history paper citation tips to keep your work professional:

  1. Never use "p." for pages: Chicago 17 drops the "p." in footnotes. Only use the numeral.
  2. Unique Titles: If each volume has a distinct title, include it in both the footnote and the bibliography.
  3. Series vs. Volumes: Do not confuse a multi-volume work with a book series. A series usually involves separate authors, whereas a multi-volume work is typically authored by the same person or team.
  4. Archival Clarity: Always identify the institution. If the archive is not well known, provide the city or province, such as "Library and Archives Canada, Ottawa."
