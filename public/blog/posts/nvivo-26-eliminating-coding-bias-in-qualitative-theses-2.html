What is coding bias in qualitative research?

Coding bias occurs when a researcher’s preconceived notions, theoretical stance, or emotional state influence how they label and categorize data. In NVivo 26, this often manifests as "confirmation bias," where you unintentionally search for quotes that support your existing hypothesis while ignoring contradictory evidence.

When you are deep into a complex project, it is easy to lose objectivity. If you find your thematic structure feels forced or disconnected from the raw data, you might need to revisit your foundation. How to minimize bias using NVivo 26 workflows

To ensure transparency in data analysis, you must document your decision-making process at every stage. NVivo 26 includes features that, when used intentionally, act as safeguards against subjectivity. Follow these steps to maintain high standards of rigour:

  • Implement Memoing as a Reflexive Tool: Use the "Memos" feature in NVivo 26 to document your thought process during every coding session. By recording why you chose a specific node, you create an audit trail for your supervisor.
  • Use the "Coding Comparison" Query: If you are working with a peer-coder or supervisor, run a coding comparison. This is the most effective way to identify where your interpretations diverge from objective data patterns.
  • Establish a Clear Codebook: Before you begin, define your nodes in a dedicated codebook. When you feel the urge to create a new "emergent" node, document the rationale first to ensure you aren't just creating labels to fit a narrative.

Addressing coding drift solutions in long-term studies

Coding drift happens when your interpretation of a node evolves over the months spent on your thesis. What you meant by "social support" in Chapter 3 might differ from what you mean in Chapter 6. To combat this:

  1. Review Initial Coded Passages: Every two weeks, go back to the first five transcripts you coded. Check if your current coding style still aligns with those initial interpretations.
  2. Set "Node Rules": In NVivo 26, use the "Node Properties" to include a clear definition for every code. Consult this definition every time you apply the code to a new segment.
  3. Schedule "Blind" Coding Sessions: Re-code a small segment of data without looking at your previous work to see if the same themes emerge.

Ensuring consistency and rigour

Qualitative inquiry is an iterative process. By maintaining a rigorous audit trail, you demonstrate to your committee that your results are rooted in the data rather than your personal bias.
