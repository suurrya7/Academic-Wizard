What is the APA 7 Approach to AI-Generated Data?

The American Psychological Association (APA) has clarified that AI models—such as ChatGPT, Claude, or specialized data synthesis tools—should be treated as software. Unlike a human author, an AI does not "create" knowledge; it generates outputs based on pre-existing patterns. Therefore, you must credit the tool as a software package.

If you are using AI to generate a raw dataset for your secondary analysis, simply mentioning it in the methodology is insufficient. You must provide a formal reference that allows your readers to verify the version of the model used, as outputs can vary significantly across different model updates.

How to Reference AI Output in Research

When citing an AI-generated dataset, the goal is to provide a "trail of evidence" for your examiners. Treat the AI as the author and the software name as the title. Your reference entry should follow this structure:

  • Author: The company that created the model (e.g., OpenAI, Anthropic).
  • Date: The year of the version you accessed.
  • Title: The name of the model including the specific version number.
  • Source: The URL where the software can be accessed.

Best Practices for APA 7 Digital Media Citations

Beyond datasets, you may occasionally need to cite AI-generated diagrams or tables. These fall under the umbrella of APA 7 digital media citations. The key is to provide a parenthetical acknowledgment within the text that describes how the content was generated. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Do I need to include the full prompt I used to generate the dataset?

While not strictly required in the reference list, it is best practice to include your prompts in an appendix or a supplemental file to ensure the reproducibility of your research.

Should I cite the AI as an "Author" or "Contributor"?

In APA 7, AI is cited as a software package. You list the creator (e.g., OpenAI) as the author. Never credit the AI as a human author.

What if the AI model version has changed since I started my paper?

Always cite the specific version you used to generate the data (e.g., GPT-4o). If you updated your analysis using a newer version, you should note the difference in your methodology section.

Is it acceptable to use AI for data collection in a PhD thesis?

Acceptability depends entirely on your specific discipline and your university’s research integrity guidelines. Always consult your supervisor before using generative tools to produce research data.
