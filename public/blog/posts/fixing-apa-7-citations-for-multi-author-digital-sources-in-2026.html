What is the APA 7 Rule for Multi-Author Digital Sources?

The core principle of the APA 7th edition is consistency. When citing digital sources with multiple authors, the rules depend heavily on how many contributors are listed. Unlike earlier versions of the manual, APA 7 streamlines the process to reduce clutter in your in-text citations.

  • One to two authors: Always name both in every citation.
  • Three or more authors: Use the surname of the first author followed by "et al." from the very first in-text citation.

For your reference list, the rules are more granular. You must list up to 20 authors. If there are 21 or more, you list the first 19, an ellipsis, and then the final author. How to Cite Complex Digital Media

Digital sources often lack traditional publication dates or clear author bylines. When dealing with web-based content, you must search for the most reliable information available. Follow these steps to ensure your APA 7 reference formatting remains compliant:

  1. Identify the Author(s): Look for individual names. If no individuals are listed, identify the group or organisational author.
  2. Determine the Date: Use the specific publication date if available. If the content is designed to be updated (like a wiki), use "n.d." and provide a retrieval date if the content is likely to change.
  3. The Title: Place the title in italics if it is a standalone source (like a report or a website page).
  4. Source/Site Name: Provide the name of the website or platform, followed by the URL.

Using a free citation generator can provide a useful draft, but always perform a manual check for complex digital media, as automated systems sometimes misinterpret multi-author metadata on dynamic platforms.

Avoiding Common APA 7th Edition Multi-Author Digital Citations Errors

Graduate students frequently fall into the trap of using "et al." too early or failing to account for corporate authors. Another common oversight is failing to distinguish between the author of a webpage and the site hosting it. For example, if a blog post is authored by an individual but hosted on a corporate site, the individual takes the primary author spot.

Final Tips for Your 2026 Bibliography

Before you finalise your document, consider these final "sanity checks" to ensure your reference list is submission-ready:

  • Check Your URLs: Ensure all digital links are active and formatted according to APA standards—remove hyperlinks if your institutional guidelines require plain text.
  • Verify Group Authors: Ensure that corporate or group names are written out in full unless an established abbreviation is common.
  • Check for Consistency: Ensure the formatting of your digital sources matches your print sources in terms of indentation and punctuation.
