What is an AI detection false positive in academic writing?

An AI detection false positive occurs when an algorithm incorrectly flags human-authored text as being generated by a Large Language Model (LLM) like ChatGPT. These detectors function by analyzing patterns, such as "perplexity" (the complexity of the text) and "burstiness" (the variation in sentence structure).

When you write in a highly structured, academic, or formal tone, your writing may inadvertently align with the probabilistic patterns these tools associate with AI. Factors that often trigger these flags include:

  • Highly consistent, formulaic sentence structures.
  • Use of standard academic vocabulary or transition phrases.
  • Technical writing that requires precise, repetitive terminology.
  • Over-reliance on common passive voice constructions.

Proving original student writing: The evidentiary trail

When defending your original work, evidence is your greatest ally. Universities generally understand that software is fallible, but they require proof that the work is yours. You should prepare a "Writing Portfolio" that demonstrates your process from start to finish.

Consider the following steps to build your defense:

  1. Version History: Most word processors (like Google Docs or Word) store extensive edit histories. Export or provide screenshots showing the chronological development of your draft over several days.
  2. Draft Iterations: Keep your rough notes, outlines, and early, messy drafts. AI rarely produces "messy" versions that follow a linear path of improvement.
  3. Source Integration: AI often hallucinates sources. If you have clearly linked your arguments to specific page numbers, journal articles, and archival materials, it is much easier to prove your human-led research process.
  4. Self-Correction: Show instances where you edited your own grammar or flow. AI doesn't typically leave traces of self-revision.

Turnitin similarity report tips for students

Many students confuse AI detection with the standard plagiarism (similarity) report. It is crucial to distinguish between the two. While plagiarism reports identify matching text from databases, AI reports identify predicted probability.
