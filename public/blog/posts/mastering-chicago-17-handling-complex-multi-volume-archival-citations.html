What is the Chicago 17 archival footnote format?

The Chicago 17 archival footnote format requires a specific sequence of information to ensure that your reader—or supervisor—can locate the exact folder, box, or item you consulted. Unlike a published book, an archival source is defined by its institutional home and its physical location within that institution.

To construct a proper footnote for a multi-volume archival series, you should typically follow this order:

  • Collection Name (if applicable)
  • Series Number or Name
  • Box or Folder Number
  • Specific Item Description (e.g., Letter, Diary Entry, Report)
  • Date of the Item
  • Name of the Repository (Archives or Library)
  • Location of the Repository (City, State/Country)

If you are citing a multi-volume collection, ensure you specify the volume or part number clearly within the descriptive element of your note. This precision is vital for history students dealing with extensive state records or private correspondence collections.

How to approach complex Chicago style citation rules

Complex Chicago style citation rules often trip up even the most diligent researchers when they encounter internal subdivisions. If a volume has its own title, it should be treated with the same weight as a book title. If the volume is simply numbered (e.g., "Volume 4"), it follows the archive's specific filing system.

Best practices for formatting archival research papers

Formatting archival research papers requires an eye for detail. Consistency is your greatest ally. Once you decide on a way to abbreviate a frequently cited archive, such as the National Archives of Australia (NAA), ensure you define that abbreviation in your first footnote.

Consider these essential tips for your thesis:

  • Be specific: If you are citing a multi-volume set held within a single box, specify both the box number and the volume number to make the archivist's job easier should someone follow your research path.
  • Use standard abbreviations: Familiarise yourself with common archival abbreviations. Avoid inventing your own, as this can confuse examiners.
  • Italics vs. Quotes: Remember that unpublished material is generally placed in quotation marks, while the names of larger collections remain in roman text unless they are a published volume series.
  • The "First Citation" Rule: Provide the full citation for the first occurrence. Subsequent mentions can be shortened to the collection name, box/folder number, and document identifier.
