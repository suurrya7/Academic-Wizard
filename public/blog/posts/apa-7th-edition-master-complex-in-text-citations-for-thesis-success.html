What is the APA 7th edition approach to multi-author sources?

One of the most frequent errors students encounter involves the treatment of APA 7 in-text citation rules for multi-author sources. Unlike previous editions, APA 7 streamlines the process to reduce clutter, but it requires strict adherence to specific thresholds.

  • One or two authors: Always cite both names every time you refer to the source (e.g., Smith & Jones, 2024).
  • Three or more authors: Use the surname of the first author followed by "et al." from the very first citation. Do not list all authors in the text.

How to handle secondary sources and government reports

Dissertation research often involves citing government reports or foundational studies found within other texts. These "secondary sources" require extra care. According to APA 7 style for secondary sources, you should only cite the work you have actually read. If you are citing a source mentioned in another work, name the original author in your sentence and provide the secondary source in the parentheses.

Example of secondary source citation:

Johnson (as cited in Miller, 2023) argued that policy reform is necessary.

For government reports, which often lack individual authors, you must treat the government department or organisation as the "author." The APA 7 reference formatting guide suggests writing the full name of the agency as the author, followed by the year. If the agency is well-known, you may introduce an abbreviation in your first citation for subsequent use.

Mastering in-text citation punctuation rules

The technicalities of punctuation are where many students lose unnecessary marks. Adhering to in-text citation punctuation rules is non-negotiable for a professional submission.

  1. Always place the full stop after the closing parenthesis of the citation, not before.
  2. Use an ampersand (&) inside parenthetical citations, but use the word "and" when naming authors in the flow of your narrative sentence.
  3. Ensure that commas separate the author, year, and page/paragraph numbers within the parentheses.

Ensuring consistency across your thesis

Consistency is the hallmark of a high-quality dissertation. Whether you are citing government reports APA 7 style or integrating complex multi-author journal articles, your internal citation format must remain uniform from the introduction to the conclusion. Small shifts in punctuation or variations in the use of "et al." can distract an examiner from the strength of your arguments.

Always perform a final audit. Search your document for "et al." to ensure it has been italicised correctly (it is a Latin abbreviation, after all) and that the period follows the "al." consistently. By paying attention to these granular details, you demonstrate a level of rigour that is expected at the doctoral and master's level.
