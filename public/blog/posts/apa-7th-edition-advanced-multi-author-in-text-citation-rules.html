Understanding the "Three-or-More" Rule in APA 7

One of the most significant changes introduced in the seventh edition was the simplification of how we cite sources with multiple contributors. Under previous versions, you were often required to list every name upon the first mention. Now, the protocol is streamlined to save space and improve readability.

For any source with three or more authors, you must shorten the citation to the first author's surname followed by "et al." from the very first mention. This applies to both parenthetical and narrative citations throughout your entire document.

  • Parenthetical example: (Smith et al., 2023)
  • Narrative example: Smith et al. (2023) noted that...

How to Handle Ambiguity and Source Conflicts

Sometimes, two different sources with three or more authors might share the same first author and publication year. This creates a "citation loop" that can confuse your reader and flag potential errors in your bibliography. If you encounter this, you must clarify which source you are referencing by listing as many additional names as necessary to distinguish them.

Avoiding Citation Errors in Your Thesis

Citation errors are one of the most common reasons students lose marks during the final review process. Often, these errors stem from inconsistencies between the in-text citation and the reference list. Here are a few tips to ensure your APA 7th edition: fixing citation loops for complex digital archives does not become a source of stress:

  • Ensure the "et al." abbreviation is followed by a period (et al.).
  • Do not include the first author's initials in the in-text citation unless necessary to distinguish two authors with the same surname.
  • Check that the punctuation—such as the comma between the author and the year—is consistent.
  • Maintain alphabetical order in your reference list, even if your in-text citations appear chronologically.

Best Practices for APA 7 Formatting Complex Sources

When you are working with complex sources—such as government reports, corporate white papers, or archival data—the rules for authors can shift. If no individual author is listed, use the group author (the organisation) as the citation. If the group author is well-known by its acronym, you may introduce the acronym in your first citation and use only the acronym thereafter.
