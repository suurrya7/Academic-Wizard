What are the common APA citation pitfalls?

Even seasoned researchers fall victim to APA citation pitfalls when dealing with papers authored by large teams. The most frequent errors occur when students fail to differentiate between the first in-text citation and subsequent ones, or when they incorrectly truncate author lists.

Common mistakes include:

  • Using "et al." too early in the citation process.
  • Failing to provide full author lists in the reference entry for papers with 21 or more authors.
  • Inconsistent use of ampersands versus the word "and" between in-text and narrative citations.
  • Mismanaging punctuation, such as missing periods after "al" in "et al."

How to handle in-text citation guidelines for multiple authors

The rules for in-text citations depend primarily on the number of authors and the flow of your sentence. APA 7th edition is designed to be streamlined, prioritising clarity for the reader while reducing clutter.

Citations with One or Two Authors

For one or two authors, always include the surname(s) every time the source is cited. Use an ampersand (&) in parenthetical citations and the word "and" in narrative citations.

Citations with Three or More Authors

This is where many researchers struggle. For a source with three or more authors, you should use the first author's surname followed by "et al." from the very first citation, both in narrative and parenthetical formats.

For instance, a paper by Smith, Jones, and Williams (2024) becomes (Smith et al., 2024) immediately. This rule applies regardless of whether the citation is the first or the fifth mention in your text.

Scholarly formatting help: The reference list rules

Your reference list is the map of your research journey. When crafting your entry for multi-author works, the academic reference list rules require precision. For sources with two to 20 authors, list all authors using the format: Surname, Initials, Surname, Initials, & Surname, Initials.

Handling 21 or More Authors

For sources with 21 or more authors, list the first 19 authors, then insert an ellipsis (...), and add the final author’s name. Do not use an ampersand before the final name in this specific scenario. It is a niche rule, but missing it can lead to unnecessary friction during peer review.
