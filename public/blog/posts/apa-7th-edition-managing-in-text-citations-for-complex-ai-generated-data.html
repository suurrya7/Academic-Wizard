What is the current guidance for citing AI in APA 7?

The American Psychological Association has clarified that AI-generated content is not a source in the traditional sense. Because ChatGPT and similar LLMs do not produce reproducible, fixed content, they do not function like a journal article or a book. Instead, the APA recommends treating AI outputs as "software" or personal communication, depending on the nature of your institution's policy.

If you are using AI to generate data or code, you must document it. Transparency ensures that your readers can distinguish between your original intellectual contributions and the assisted outputs. How to cite an LLM in an APA 7-compliant paper

When you incorporate LLM-generated data, your citation must provide enough information for a reader to understand that the output was synthetic. The standard format for citing an LLM is as follows:

  • Author: OpenAI (or the specific developer).
  • Date: Year of the version used.
  • Title: Name of the model (e.g., ChatGPT-4o).
  • Version: The specific model version number.
  • Type: [Large language model].
  • URL: The direct link to the service.

In-text, you should clearly signal that the content was AI-generated. For example: "According to the analysis generated by ChatGPT (OpenAI, 2026), the correlation between variables X and Y..." If the output is lengthy, it is best practice to append the full transcript to your paper as an appendix rather than citing it within the body alone.

Ethics of AI in research: Maintaining your integrity

The ethics of AI in research goes beyond mere citation. While tools can assist in summarising literature or organizing data frames, they cannot replace the critical, human-led synthesis required for a doctoral thesis or high-impact paper. Over-reliance on AI can lead to "hallucinations" or biased data, which can trigger institutional integrity alerts.

How to document AI-assisted methodology

Documenting AI-assisted work should be transparent and descriptive. You should explain the role the AI played in your project, such as:

  • Refining research questions or hypotheses.
  • Assisting in data cleaning or syntax generation.
  • Summarising large volumes of unstructured qualitative text.
  • Simulating data scenarios for pilot testing.

Always state the prompt you used if the AI’s output is central to your findings. By providing the exact input, you allow peers to replicate your research process—a core tenet of the scientific method.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Should I list the AI tool in my bibliography? Yes. According to APA guidelines, if the software is central to your research findings, it must be included in your reference list to provide a clear audit trail.

Is citing AI enough to avoid plagiarism? No. Citing AI identifies the source, but it does not validate the content. You must still perform your own critical analysis and verify all facts, or you risk academic misconduct.

What if the AI output is not publicly accessible? If the output was generated through a private account, mention this in your methodology section and provide the prompt as an appendix so that the data can be audited if requested by your supervisor.

Does APA 7 allow for AI-generated paraphrasing? While not explicitly banned, using AI to paraphrase your own work often strips it of your unique "academic voice," which is essential for doctoral-level assessment. Use it for structure, not for final wording.
