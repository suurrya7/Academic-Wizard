What is Coding Bias in Qualitative Research?

Coding bias occurs when a researcher’s preconceived notions, theoretical framework, or emotional investment in the outcome influences the categorisation of raw data. In doctoral research, this often manifests as "confirmation bias," where you unintentionally search for evidence that supports your hypothesis while ignoring contradictory themes.

In the context of NVivo, this can lead to "coding drift," where the definition of a theme shifts over the course of the analysis. If you start coding your data in January and finish in June, the criteria you used for your first five interviews may be subtly different from your final five. Addressing NVivo 26: Debugging Qualitative Coding for PhD Theses is a critical step in standardising your approach.

How to Identify and Manage Coding Drift

Coding drift is a common challenge in NVivo 26: Practical Workflow to Reduce Researcher Coding Bias. To ensure your qualitative thesis remains robust, consider these technical strategies:

  • Codebook Documentation: Maintain a living document of your node definitions. Every time you refine a node description, log it with a date stamp.
  • Inter-coder Reliability Checks: If possible, have a peer or supervisor code a sample of your data. Compare your results using NVivo’s comparison tools to identify discrepancies.
  • The "Reflexive Journal": Keep a separate memo folder in NVivo where you document your thoughts before, during, and after coding sessions.
  • Frequency Audits: Use NVivo’s query tools to review how often you are assigning specific codes to unexpected data points.
