What is the APA 7th Edition Multi-Author Rule?

The transition from APA 6 to 7 brought significant simplifications. One of the most helpful changes involves how we treat sources with three or more authors. In previous versions, you often had to list every name upon the first mention. That is no longer the case.

Under the current standards, for any source with three or more authors, you truncate the citation to the first author's surname followed by "et al." from the very first mention in your paper. This shift aims to reduce word clutter and improve the flow of academic prose.

  • Parenthetical citation: (Smith et al., 2023)
  • Narrative citation: Smith et al. (2023) argued that...

How to Manage Complex Academic Citation Rules

While the "et al." rule is straightforward, complex academic citation rules often arise when you have sources with identical first authors or when dealing with ambiguous group authors.

Handling Multiple Sources with the Same "et al."

What happens if you have two different sources that both cite "Smith et al." from 2023? Simply writing "Smith et al. (2023)" for both creates confusion for your reader and the examiner. In this instance, you must add as many author names as necessary to distinguish between the two sources.

If the first two authors are the same, include the third author's name, and so on, until the distinction is clear. If you struggle to keep your sources organized as your document grows, look into a Zotero workflow to maintain integrity across hundreds of references.

Avoiding Citation Penalties in University Papers

To ensure you aren't losing points on your final submission, you need to understand the relationship between in-text citations and your reference list. Academic markers look for a one-to-one correspondence. Every source cited in your body text must appear in your reference list, and every reference must be cited at least once.

Common pitfalls include:

  • Missing periods: Note that "et al." always includes a period after the "al." because it is an abbreviation for et alii.
  • Italicizing incorrectly: Never italicize "et al." in your in-text citations.
  • Punctuation placement: In parenthetical citations, the period always comes after the closing parenthesis.

Referencing Digital Sources in APA 7

In the digital age, we frequently cite websites, social media, and interactive data sets. These sources often lack clear authorship. If you are struggling with content that feels modern but doesn't fit standard templates, remember that the goal of APA is always clear communication.

When an author is unknown, use the title of the work in the citation. If the author is an organization (like Statistics Canada or a government agency), list the organization as the author. For digital sources, always prioritize the URL and the publication date, or "n.d." if no date is available.
