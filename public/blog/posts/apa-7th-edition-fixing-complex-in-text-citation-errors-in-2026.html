How to Handle Multi-Author Citation Pitfalls

The most common source of confusion in the APA 7th edition remains the treatment of multiple authors. Unlike older editions that required listing every name under certain conditions, APA 7 simplifies the process while introducing specific triggers that students frequently miss.

Managing Three or More Authors

For any source with three or more authors, you must use the "et al." abbreviation from the very first mention. There is no "first mention" grace period where you list everyone. Here is how to format it correctly:

  • Parenthetical: (Smith et al., 2026).
  • Narrative: Smith et al. (2026) argued that...

Remember that the period after "al" is mandatory, but there is no period after "et" because it is a Latin phrase rather than an abbreviation. Missing this subtle detail is a common multi-author citation pitfall that examiners flag during review.

Handling Group Authors

When citing a report by an organisation or government body, spell out the full name of the group on the first citation. If the group has a well-known abbreviation, you may include it in brackets. For subsequent citations, use the abbreviation alone.

  • Dates: Always include the year. If the content is designed to be updated (like a wiki), include a retrieval date.

What is the Correct Order for Citations?

When you have multiple works by the same author, or need to cite multiple sources in a single parenthetical, the order is strictly defined by the APA 7th edition in-text citation rules. Failure to follow this order is a common formatting penalty that is entirely avoidable.

  1. Same Author, Different Years: Chronological order, separated by commas (e.g., Jones, 2020, 2022).
  2. Different Authors: Alphabetical order by the first author's surname, separated by semicolons (e.g., Adams, 2021; Brown, 2023).
  3. Same Author, Same Year: Order them alphabetically by title (e.g., Smith, 2024a, 2024b).
