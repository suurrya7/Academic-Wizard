What is the core rule for APA 7th edition multi-author in-text citation rules?

The most important shift in the seventh edition is the immediate use of "et al." for works with three or more authors. In the sixth edition, you were often required to list all names the first time you cited a source. Now, you abbreviate from the very first mention.

This streamlining helps reduce visual clutter in your paragraphs. However, because it is so different from older styles, it remains one of the most frequent citation common mistakes. How to format one or two authors

For papers with one or two authors, the rule remains consistent and straightforward:

  • One author: Include the surname and the year (e.g., Smith, 2024).
  • Two authors: Always include both surnames every time the work is cited (e.g., Smith & Jones, 2024). Use the ampersand (&) inside parentheses and the word "and" in the narrative flow.

How to format three or more authors

When you are referencing a source with three or more authors, the formatting changes to favour brevity:

  • First and subsequent citations: Provide only the surname of the first author followed by "et al." and the year (e.g., Brown et al., 2023).
  • Note: Do not include a comma before "et al." in an in-text citation, but do place a period after "al." because it is an abbreviation of the Latin et alii.

Common pitfalls in APA 7 reference formatting

Even with clear rules, students often struggle with ambiguity when multiple sources have the same first author and publication year. If you have two different sources, both by "Smith et al. (2023)," you must distinguish between them to prevent confusion.

To differentiate these, add as many subsequent author names as needed until the citations are distinct. If adding more names does not solve the issue, append a suffix (a, b, c) to the year: (Smith et al., 2023a) and (Smith et al., 2023b).

Handling group authors

Sometimes your source isn't an individual, but a government agency or an organisation. The rules for group authors with an abbreviation are as follows:

  • First citation: Write out the full name of the group. If the group has a well-known abbreviation, include it in square brackets (e.g., [ABS], 2023).
  • Subsequent citations: You may use just the abbreviation (ABS, 2023).
