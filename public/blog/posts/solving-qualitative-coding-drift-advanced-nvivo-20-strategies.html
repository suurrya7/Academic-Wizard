What is Coding Drift in Qualitative Research?

Coding drift occurs when the criteria you established for your nodes at the beginning of your analysis evolve as you grow more familiar with your data. Without a structured approach, you might start applying a label to a passage in month six that you would have excluded in month two. This inconsistency undermines the rigour of your findings and, ultimately, the credibility of your thesis.

How to Maintain Consistency in NVivo 20

Maintaining analytical integrity requires a robust plan. NVivo 20 offers sophisticated features to help you stay on track, but the software is only as consistent as the researcher using it. To succeed in preventing thematic coding bias in NVivo 20, follow these expert-level workflows:

  • Develop a Dynamic Codebook: Never rely on memory. Your codebook should contain clear definitions, inclusion criteria, and exclusion criteria for every node.
  • Implement Inter-Coder Reliability (ICR) Checks: Even if you are a solo researcher, you can conduct a "self-check" by re-coding a small sample of previously analysed data after a two-week break.
  • Utilise Matrix Coding Queries: Regularly run queries to compare code distribution across different demographics or time periods to identify anomalies.
  • Annotate Rigorously: Use the 'Annotations' feature in NVivo 20 to explain why you applied a specific node. This creates a reflexive audit trail that is invaluable during the writing stage.
