What is Ethical AI Scaffolding?

Ethical AI scaffolding refers to using Large Language Models (LLMs) to structure, categorize, and conceptualize your research data while you remain the primary author of the content. It is the practice of delegating the "heavy lifting" of organization to AI, while keeping the critical evaluation of scholarly sources strictly in your own hands.

How to Use AI Ethically for Literature Review

Using AI for AI for academic literature synthesis requires a disciplined approach to prompt engineering. Instead of asking an AI to "write my literature review," use it to help you organize your existing notes and source summaries. Here is a step-by-step ethical framework:

  • Categorize Themes: Input your own summaries of selected papers and ask the AI: "Identify the primary thematic overlaps and gaps in these notes."
  • Drafting Outlines: Request a logical literature review structure guide based on the themes you have already identified in your research.
  • Refining Arguments: Use AI to critique your existing logical flow, asking: "Where does this argument lack empirical support from the sources provided?"
  • Citation Management: Always cross-reference your AI-generated structure with your bibliography. If you find your metadata becoming disorganized, use a Zotero Library Clean-up: Solving Duplicate Metadata in 2026 to keep your foundation solid.

Ethical AI Prompt Engineering for Students

The key to success lies in the quality of your input. Avoid "black box" prompts. Instead, use transparent, structured prompts that act as a mirror to your own research process. When practicing ethical AI prompt engineering for students, consider these best practices:

  1. Declare Your Intent: Tell the AI, "I am a graduate student conducting a literature review on [Topic]. Act as an academic mentor to help me refine my outline."
  2. Provide Context: Always provide the AI with your specific research question and the actual abstracts you are working with.
  3. Ask for Logic, Not Words: Focus your prompts on structure, clarity, and logical consistency rather than prose.
  4. Maintain Control: Never copy and paste AI-generated text directly into your draft. Use it as a structural prompt for your own original writing.
