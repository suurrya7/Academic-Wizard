Why Does Human-Written Text Get Flagged as AI?

Turnitin’s AI writing detection tool identifies patterns that it associates with Large Language Models (LLMs). While these tools are sophisticated, they are not infallible. High false-positive rates occur because human writing—especially in academic contexts—often follows predictable structures that AI also mimics.

Common triggers for false positives include:

  • Predictable structure: Formal academic English often utilizes repetitive, high-probability word sequences that AI models are trained to prioritize.
  • Non-native language usage: Students writing in a second language may use standardized phrasing that mimics the "average" probability patterns detected by AI.
  • Extensive citations: Heavy reliance on technical terminology and quotes can confuse detection algorithms, which struggle to distinguish between a student’s voice and the source material.
