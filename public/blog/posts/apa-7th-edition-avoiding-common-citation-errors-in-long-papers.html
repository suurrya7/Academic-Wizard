How to fix common in-text citation errors

In-text citations are the most frequent point of failure in long papers. Because they appear so often, even a small systemic error—like using an ampersand (&) incorrectly—can lead to dozens of points being docked. Here are the most common mistakes:

  • The Ampersand Error: In parenthetical citations, use an ampersand (&) between names. In narrative citations, always use the word "and".
  • Three or More Authors: Forget "et al." for the first mention. Under APA 7, use "et al." from the very first citation for any source with three or more authors.
  • Missing Page Numbers for Quotes: If you are quoting directly, you must include a page number (e.g., p. 24) or a paragraph number (para. 3).
  • Year Omission: Ensure every citation includes the year, even if the author's name is repeated multiple times in the same paragraph.

Bibliography formatting help for long-form papers

The reference list at the end of your document is often the final thing a marker checks. A messy bibliography gives the impression of a rushed conclusion. To keep your work polished, pay attention to these specific areas:

1. Hanging Indents

APA 7 requires a hanging indent for every reference entry. This means the first line is flush to the left, and subsequent lines are indented. Do not try to achieve this by hitting "Enter" and "Tab" manually; use your word processor's paragraph settings to apply this automatically.

2. Capitalisation Rules

Titles of articles and books in the reference list should use sentence case for the article or book title itself, but keep the journal name and volume number in title case. This is a common point of confusion that separates high-achieving papers from the rest.

3. DOI and URL Formatting

In the 7th edition, DOIs and URLs are presented as hyperlinks. You no longer need to write "Retrieved from" unless a retrieval date is necessary (usually only for sources that change over time, like a Wikipedia entry).

Checklist for APA 7 Compliance

To avoid losing marks on your next major submission, perform this quick audit:

  1. Margins: Are all margins set to 2.54cm (1 inch) on all sides?
  2. Font: Are you using a standard font like Calibri 11pt, Arial 11pt, or Times New Roman 12pt?
  3. Line Spacing: Is your entire paper, including references, double-spaced?
  4. Page Numbers: Are they in the top-right header, beginning with the title page?
  5. Reference Sort: Is your reference list sorted alphabetically by the first author's surname?
  6. Consistency: Do all works cited in the text appear in the reference list, and vice versa?

By following these steps, you demonstrate that you have respected the academic conventions of your field. APA 7 is not just about rules; it is about facilitating the ease of communication between researchers. When you master these conventions, you allow your research to stand out for its content, rather than its formatting faults.
