What is the general rule for multi-author citations in APA 7th edition?

The APA 7th edition simplified the requirements for citing sources with multiple authors to improve readability. The goal is to avoid cluttering your text with long strings of names while still providing credit where due.

  • One or two authors: Always list both names every time you cite the source.
  • Three or more authors: You must shorten the citation to the first author's surname followed by "et al." from the very first mention.

For example, if you are citing a paper by Smith, Jones, and Brown (2024), your in-text citation should be written as (Smith et al., 2024). Note that "et al." is an abbreviation for the Latin et alii, meaning "and others." It is essential to include the period after "al" but not after "et."

How to cite 3+ authors in APA 7th edition correctly

Learning how to cite 3+ authors in APA correctly is essential for maintaining a clean, professional flow. The transition to using "et al." for all sources with three or more authors regardless of the frequency of mention is a major shift from previous editions.

When incorporating these citations into your prose, keep these formatting tips in mind:

  • Parenthetical citation: (Smith et al., 2024).
  • Narrative citation: Smith et al. (2024) argued that...

Managing complex citations: Avoiding ambiguity

Occasionally, you may encounter two different sources that, when shortened to "et al.," result in identical in-text citations. This can happen if the first authors are the same and the sources were published in the same year. In these rare instances, APA protocol dictates that you must list as many additional authors as necessary to distinguish between the two sources.

Best practices for citation managers

While software like Zotero or Mendeley is invaluable, they are not infallible. "Garbage in, garbage out" is a common problem in academic research. If your initial data import is flawed, your citations will be incorrect. Always check:

  1. That all author names are correctly spelled in your software database.
  2. That the publication year is accurate.
  3. That the software is set to the correct citation style—ensure it is specifically "APA 7th Edition."
