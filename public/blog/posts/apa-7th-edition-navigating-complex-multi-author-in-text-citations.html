What is the APA 7th Edition Rule for Three or More Authors?

One of the most common mistakes students make involves the treatment of sources with multiple contributors. Under the APA 7th edition, the rules for shortening citations have been streamlined to improve readability.

  • For three or more authors: You must shorten the citation to the first author’s surname followed by "et al." from the very first time it appears in the text.
  • Example: (Smith et al., 2024).
  • Note: You must list all authors in the reference list if there are up to 20 authors.

How to Handle Ambiguous Multi-Author Citations

Sometimes, multiple sources might shorten to the same "et al." citation, creating confusion. APA style has specific protocols to avoid ambiguity. If you have two different sources that would both result in the same "et al." citation, you must list as many authors as necessary to distinguish between them.

For example, if you are citing Smith, Jones, Brown, and Miller (2023) and Smith, Jones, Green, and Taylor (2023), you cannot simply use "Smith et al." for both. You must list the first two authors to provide clarity for the reader.

What is the Correct Approach to Group Authors?

When citing works by organisations, government departments, or non-profit groups, the rules change slightly. If the group name is long and has a common abbreviation, you should spell out the full name the first time it is cited, followed by the abbreviation in square brackets.

Example: (National Health and Medical Research Council [NHMRC], 2025). Subsequent citations can simply use (NHMRC, 2025).

  3. Update your Zotero style repository to ensure you are running the latest version of the APA 7th edition CSL file.
