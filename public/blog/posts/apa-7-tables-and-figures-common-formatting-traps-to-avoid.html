Common Traps in APA 7 Visual Element Guidelines

Many students confuse the rules for tables and figures. Because they serve different purposes, the layout requirements differ significantly. Avoiding common mistakes is the first step in fixing citation penalties that accumulate over a long document.

Here are the most frequent errors encountered during the review process:

  • Inconsistent Numbering: Tables and figures should be numbered sequentially (e.g., Table 1, Table 2). Never use a mix of styles like "Table 1a."
  • Misplaced Captions: One of the most critical aspects of apa 7 caption placement is understanding that the table title appears above the table, while the figure caption appears below the figure.
  • Vertical Lines: APA 7 style strictly prohibits vertical lines within tables. Only use horizontal lines to separate column headers and the table body.
  • Font Discrepancy: Ensure your table content matches the typeface used in the body of your paper, such as Times New Roman 12pt or Arial 11pt.

How to Format Tables Correctly

Tables are designed for clarity and conciseness. When presenting complex data, simplicity is your best ally. Follow this structural blueprint for every table:

  1. Table Number: Bold, flush left, placed above the table.
  2. Table Title: Italicized, title case, double-spaced below the table number.
  3. The Table: Use only horizontal lines. No vertical borders.
  4. Table Note: Indented like a paragraph, beginning with the word "Note." in italics.

How to Format Figures Correctly

Figures include charts, graphs, maps, and photographs. The layout rules for figures prioritize the image itself, with the supporting text placed directly underneath.

To adhere to standard professional academic formatting, ensure you follow these steps:

  • Place the figure number above the image, flush left, in bold.
  • Place the figure title, double-spaced and italicized, below the number.
  • Place the visual element itself, centered or flush left.
  • Include the caption/legend below the figure, which provides necessary context and credit for the source.

Best Practices for Success

To ensure your paper passes the eye test for your 2026 assignments, adopt these habits:

  • Check Alignment: Ensure all numbers in columns are aligned by decimal point for easier comparison.
  • Keep Captions Brief: A caption should explain what the figure shows without repeating the information already present in your main text.
  • Source Attribution: If a figure is adapted or copied from elsewhere, ensure the credit line is included in the note below the figure.
  • Check for Consistency: Use the same style for all captions throughout the entire paper.
