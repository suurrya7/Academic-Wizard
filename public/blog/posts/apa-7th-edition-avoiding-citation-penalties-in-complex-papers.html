Navigating APA 7 Multi-Author In-Text Citation Rules

The most common area where students lose marks is the inconsistent application of multi-author citations. While APA 7 simplified these rules, "formatting traps" remain rampant in long-form writing. Understanding the threshold for when to use "et al." is essential to keeping your manuscript polished.

  • One or two authors: Always include both names in every citation (e.g., Smith & Jones, 2025).
  • Three or more authors: Use the first author’s surname followed by "et al." from the very first citation. Do not list all authors in the first instance.
  • Group authors with abbreviations: If a group author (like Health Canada) is well-known, write it out fully the first time, including the abbreviation in brackets. Use only the abbreviation for subsequent citations.

How to Manage Complex Digital Source Citation

Modern research often involves non-traditional sources, from social media posts to interactive databases. APA 7 has specific protocols for these, yet many students continue to apply legacy formatting. For a deep dive into these requirements, see our guide on APA 7th Edition In-Text Citation Rules for Complex Digital Media.

When citing digital media:

  • Missing dates: Use "n.d." if the source provides no publication date.
  • Social media: Include the screen name and the real name if available.
  • DOIs and URLs: Present DOIs as hyperlinks (https://doi.org/...). For URLs, ensure they are live, clickable links in your final digital submission.

APA 7 Tables and Figures Guide

Tables and figures should be treated as visual summaries that complement, not repeat, your text. In Canadian academic standards, clarity and professionalism are prioritized. Misaligning your table numbers or misplacing your "Note" section is a frequent reason for point deductions. To avoid these common pitfalls, review our expert advice on APA 7 Tables & Figures: Avoiding Common Formatting Penalties.

Key rules to remember for your visuals include:

  1. Table Number: Bolded, appearing above the table.
  2. Title: Descriptive and italicized, appearing one double-spaced line below the number.
  3. Notes: Use "Note." in italics to explain abbreviations or copyright attribution directly under the table.

Common APA 7 Formatting Traps to Avoid

Beyond citations, several "hidden" traps can jeopardize your grade. Being aware of these will save you hours of revision time:

  • The Reference Page: Ensure a hanging indent is applied to all entries. Many word processors default to first-line indents, which is a major error in APA 7.
  • Block Quotations: Any quote of 40 words or more must be set as a free-standing block of text, indented 0.5 inches, without quotation marks.
  • Running Heads: APA 7 no longer requires "Running head:" on the title page for student papers, unless your professor specifically requests it. Check your department’s unique guidelines.
