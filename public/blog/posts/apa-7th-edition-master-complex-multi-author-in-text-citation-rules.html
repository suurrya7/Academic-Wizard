What is the core rule for multi-author citations in APA 7?

The APA 7th edition significantly simplified how we handle sources with three or more authors. Unlike older versions, you no longer need to list every single author the first time you mention a source.

The standard rule is simple: for a work with three or more authors, use the surname of the first author followed by "et al." for every in-text citation, including the very first time you mention the source.

  • Parenthetical citation: (Brown et al., 2024)
  • Narrative citation: Brown et al. (2024) noted that...

This approach keeps your sentences clean and prevents your text from becoming cluttered with long lists of names. Always ensure the year follows the "et al." abbreviation.

How to handle APA 7 reference formatting for complex cases

While the "et al." rule covers the vast majority of cases, specific scenarios require careful attention. If you are struggling with unique sources, our APA 7th Edition: Fixing Complex In-Text Citation Errors in 2026 guide offers deeper insights into resolving edge cases.

Consider these formatting nuances for your 2026 research projects:

  • Multiple works, same author, same year: If you have two papers by the same group published in the same year, distinguish them by adding a letter after the year (e.g., 2024a, 2024b).
  • Ambiguous citations: If two different sources with different authors both shorten to the same "et al." citation, add as many names as needed to distinguish them before adding "et al."
  • Grouping citations: When citing multiple works in the same parentheses, order them alphabetically by the first author's surname and separate them with a semicolon.

Avoiding common APA 7 citation errors

Even seasoned researchers fall into traps regarding correct citation style for 3+ authors. Avoiding these mistakes is crucial for polished academic output. Before submitting, review your document for the following common slips:

  1. Incorrect use of "&": Use the ampersand (&) only inside parentheses. In narrative text, always use the word "and."
  2. Forgetting the period after "al": Remember that "et al." is an abbreviation for "et alii" (and others). It must always include the period after "al".
  3. Extra punctuation: Do not add a comma between the author's name and "et al." unless it is necessary to avoid confusion.
