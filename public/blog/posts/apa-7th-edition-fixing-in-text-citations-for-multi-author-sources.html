What is the core rule for APA 7 multi-author citation?

The fundamental principle of APA 7 citation is brevity combined with clarity. Unlike older styles, APA 7 streamlines in-text citations to prevent clutter, particularly when dealing with long lists of contributors. For sources with three or more authors, you should use the “et al.” convention from the very first mention. This applies regardless of whether the citation is parenthetical or narrative. For example, if you are citing a paper by Smith, Jones, Williams, and Brown, you would write (Smith et al., 2024) in the text.

How to handle single and double-author citations

Before diving into complex lists, remember the basic structure for smaller groups:

  • One author: Use the surname and the year (e.g., Thompson, 2025).
  • Two authors: Always include both surnames every time the work is cited. Use an ampersand (&) in parenthetical citations and the word "and" in narrative flow.
  • Two authors example: (Miller & Davis, 2023) or Miller and Davis (2023) argued that...

APA 7 multi-author citation rules for three or more contributors

This is where students often lose marks. APA 7 simplified these rules to improve readability. If your source has three or more authors, use the first author’s surname followed by "et al." immediately, even the first time you cite it.

Parenthetical Citations

Place the entire citation within parentheses at the end of the sentence. Remember that "et al." includes a period because "al." is an abbreviation for the Latin *alii*.

Example: Recent findings suggest that urban green spaces significantly improve mental wellbeing (Patel et al., 2026).

Narrative Citations

When you weave the authors into the sentence structure, the year remains in parentheses. Ensure the punctuation follows the author name correctly.

Example: Patel et al. (2026) demonstrated that community gardens foster social cohesion in metropolitan areas.

Addressing potential ambiguity: What if names are identical?

Sometimes, two different sources with three or more authors share the same first author and publication year. In these rare cases, simply using "et al." would make the sources indistinguishable. To resolve this, include as many of the subsequent authors' names as necessary to differentiate the works.
