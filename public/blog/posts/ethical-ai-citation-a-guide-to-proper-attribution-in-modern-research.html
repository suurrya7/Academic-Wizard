How to Cite AI Tools in Academic Papers: APA 7th Edition Rules

The American Psychological Association (APA) has clarified its position: AI models should be cited when their output is used as a source of information. Because AI is not a person, the model is treated as the author. Here is how to apply APA 7th edition AI citation rules correctly:

  • In-text citation: (Author/Company, Year). For example, (OpenAI, 2024).
  • Reference list: Name of Model (Version). (Year). Name of Product (Version number) [Large language model]. URL.

Transparent Research Methodology: Best Practices

To maintain credibility, your research methodology must be explicit. Avoid passing off machine-generated text as your own. Follow these steps to ensure your process remains ethical:

  1. Document the Tool: Clearly state which AI model (e.g., ChatGPT-4, Claude 3.5, Gemini) you utilised.
  2. State the Purpose: Explain if it was used for copy-editing, brainstorming, or data analysis.
  3. Verify Content: Always cross-reference AI-generated facts with peer-reviewed literature.
  4. Maintain Records: Keep a log of your chat history or prompts, as some UK universities now request these during the viva voce or submission review.

The Role of Human Oversight

No AI tool can replace the critical thinking and subjective nuance required in postgraduate research. Relying too heavily on AI can lead to "hallucinations"—where the model presents false information as fact. Always treat AI as a research assistant, not a co-author. If you find your text lacks the necessary human academic voice, you may need to focus on refining your writing style to match the expectations of your institution.
