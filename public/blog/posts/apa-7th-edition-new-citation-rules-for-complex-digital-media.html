How to Cite Social Media Threads Properly

Social media threads present a challenge because they often lack a formal title. Under APA 7th edition guidelines, you should provide the first 20 words of the post as the title. Here is the standard structure for a tweet or post:

  • Author, A. A. [@handle]. (Year, Month Day). The first 20 words of the post content... [Description of format]. Platform Name. URL

The "description of format" serves as a vital bracketed element. For a thread, it is helpful to indicate that the source is a "threaded post" or a "social media post." Remember that accuracy in your punctuation—specifically the period after the author’s name and the brackets—is often what differentiates a high-scoring paper from one that requires revision.

What is the Correct Format for Podcasts and Streams?

Podcasts have become a staple in academic discourse. The APA 7th edition requires you to distinguish between the host and the executive producer if both are known. The format should prioritise accessibility:

  • Host, H. H. (Host). (Year, Month Day). Title of episode (No. number) [Audio podcast episode]. In Title of podcast series. Publisher. URL

If you are citing a live-streamed event (such as a YouTube live video), ensure you include the specific channel name as the publisher. If the video is likely to be updated or has no fixed version, consider adding a retrieval date, though this is only necessary if the content is designed to change frequently.

Expert Tips for 2026 Academic Standards

As we approach the latter half of the decade, the integration of digital media into research is expected to grow. To keep your work ahead of the curve, keep these three rules in mind:

  1. Prioritise Permanent Links: If the platform provides a "share" link or a permanent URL, use that rather than your browser's current address bar, which might contain session IDs.
  2. Verify the Author: With social media, the account name (the text displayed) and the handle (the @name) are different. Always cite the account name as the author and include the handle in brackets.
  3. Contextualise Your Media: Do not just drop a link in your bibliography. Ensure your text clearly states why the digital media is relevant to your argument, reinforcing its status as an academic source.
