The Golden Rules of APA 7 Visuals

In APA 7, tables and figures serve distinct purposes. A table displays data in rows and columns, while a figure is everything else—graphs, charts, diagrams, photographs, or drawings. Both must be referred to in the text by their number (e.g., "see Table 1") rather than by phrases like "the table above."

What is the correct structure for an APA 7 table?

Tables in APA 7 are built for readability. They should be clean, open, and free of vertical lines. Follow this hierarchy for your table components:

  • Table Number: Bold, placed above the table.
  • Table Title: Italics, title case, placed one double-spaced line below the number.
  • The Table Itself: Only horizontal lines are permitted—usually at the top and bottom of the table and beneath the column headers.
  • Table Note: If needed, place this below the table in italics, starting with the word "Note."

How to Format Figures Correctly

Figures require a different approach to labeling. While table labels go above the content, figure labels and captions sit below the figure. This is a common trap where students lose points in Beyond APA 7: Navigating Citation Rules for Complex Digital Media, where image usage is prevalent.

To avoid common formatting traps, keep these steps in mind:

  1. Label placement: The label "Figure X" must be in bold.
  2. Caption placement: The descriptive title sits below the label, in italics.
  3. Consistency: Use a clear, sans-serif font like Calibri (11 pt), Arial (11 pt), or Lucida Sans Unicode (10 pt) within your images for accessibility.
  4. Resolution: Ensure your images are high quality. Blurry charts are a hallmark of rushed academic research paper formatting.

Common Formatting Traps to Avoid

Students often fail to realize that visual aids must be fully self-explanatory. A reader should understand your data without needing to consult the body text. Avoid these frequent pitfalls:

  • Overloading: Keep tables simple. If a table spans three pages, you likely need to split it or rethink your presentation.
  • Redundancy: Do not repeat all your data in the text. Your text should highlight the most significant patterns, while the table provides the granular evidence.
  • Missing Copyright Attribution: If you adapted a table or figure from another source, you must cite it in the table note using the phrase "From [Title of work], by [Author], [Year], [Publisher]. Copyright [Year] by [Copyright Holder]."
