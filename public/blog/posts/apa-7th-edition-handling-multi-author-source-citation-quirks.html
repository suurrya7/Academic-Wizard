What is the core rule for multi-author in-text citations?

Ensure you reference authoritative sources. For further reading, consult QAA Standards.

The transition to APA 7th Edition simplified many rules, yet confusion remains regarding papers with numerous contributors. The primary goal is to ensure the reader can easily identify the source while keeping the text readable.

  • One or two authors: Always cite both names in every in-text citation (e.g., Smith & Jones, 2023).
  • Three or more authors: Use the "et al." shorthand from the very first mention.

This is a significant departure from older editions, which required listing every author the first time a source appeared. How to handle APA 7 reference list formatting for many authors

While in-text citations favor brevity, your reference list requires transparency. This is where citing papers with many authors requires meticulous attention to detail. The rules depend entirely on the total number of contributors listed on the source:

  • 1 to 20 authors: List all authors in the reference list entry. Use an ampersand (&) before the final name.
  • 21 or more authors: List the first 19 authors, insert an ellipsis (but no ampersand), and then add the name of the final author.

APA 7 in-text citation examples for clarity

Understanding how these rules look on the page is essential. Here are the standard formats for parenthetical and narrative citations:

Parenthetical citation (three or more authors):

Recent research indicates that qualitative data saturation is reached earlier than previously thought (Brown et al., 2022).

Narrative citation (three or more authors):

Brown et al. (2022) argued that qualitative data saturation occurs sooner than standard literature suggests.

Notice the punctuation: "et" does not have a full stop, but "al." does. Always ensure there is a space after the "al." and before the year. Common pitfalls in academic citation guidance

One of the most frequent errors Australian students make involves the treatment of group authors or secondary sources. Remember that if the author name is the same as the group name (e.g., a university or government department), you do not need to repeat the name twice.

Furthermore, ensure you are not relying solely on automated tools. While software is helpful, the human eye is necessary for identifying "orphaned" citations where an author's name might have been omitted or spelled incorrectly.
