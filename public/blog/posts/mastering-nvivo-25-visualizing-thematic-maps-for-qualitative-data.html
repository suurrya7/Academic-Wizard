What is Thematic Mapping in NVivo 25?

Thematic mapping is the process of visually representing the relationships, hierarchies, and connections between the codes you have developed during your NVivo 25 thematic analysis guide process. In NVivo 25, these maps serve as dynamic tools to "think with" your data, allowing you to move beyond simple frequency counts toward deeper analytical insights.

How to Build Visual Thematic Maps

Once your coding is complete, NVivo 25 allows you to build sophisticated models to explore your findings. Follow this workflow to create a robust thematic model:

Step 1: Initiating a Concept Map

Navigate to the 'Explore' tab in the ribbon and select 'Concept Map'. This opens a canvas where you can drag and drop your nodes. The beauty of this feature is its fluidity; you can experiment with moving nodes to see if a central theme (e.g., "Participant Autonomy") holds the weight you initially hypothesised.

Step 2: Defining Relationships

Use the 'Relationship' tool to draw lines between your nodes. Label these relationships clearly—for example, "contributes to," "conflicts with," or "is a prerequisite for." This forces you to articulate the theoretical connections that underpin your structuring your dissertation: a 2026 guide to thesis synthesis and makes your argument more persuasive.

Step 3: Exporting for Your Dissertation

Once your map is complete, use the 'Export' function to save your map as a high-resolution image file. Ensure that all labels are legible. If the map feels cluttered, it is often an indicator that your thematic structure is too broad; consider breaking it into two distinct figures to enhance readability.
