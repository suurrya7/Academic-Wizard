How to handle digital source attribution for multiple authors

When citing websites APA 7 style, the author count dictates how you format the in-text citation. Understanding these rules is essential to avoiding APA citation penalties that often stem from minor formatting oversights.

  • One author: (Author Surname, Year).
  • Two authors: (Surname1 & Surname2, Year). Use an ampersand (&) inside parentheses.
  • Three or more authors: (Surname1 et al., Year). Note that "et al." is Latin for "and others." It is always followed by a period.

How to manage complex online sources effectively

Digital media often presents unique challenges, such as missing dates or group authors. If a website does not list an individual, look for a group author or organization. If no author is available, use the title of the article or page in place of the author's name. For titles, use double quotation marks for short works or italicize for web pages or reports.

Avoiding common pitfalls in digital citation

Even seasoned researchers fall into traps when formatting digital sources. Here are three common mistakes to watch out for:

  1. Overusing "et al.": Ensure you use "et al." only for works with three or more authors. Do not use it for two authors, even in later citations.
  2. Missing URLs: In APA 7, you must include the full URL or DOI at the end of your reference entry. Always ensure these links are active and functional.
  3. Confusing the Date: If a website is updated frequently, use the "last updated" date. If the site is meant to change (like a wiki), include a retrieval date.

By staying vigilant, you protect your academic voice and show your markers that you have meticulously accounted for every source of information. Precision in your references acts as a hallmark of high-quality research.
