What is the core rule for in-text citations?

In APA 7th edition, the primary goal for in-text citations is clarity and brevity. The rules depend entirely on the number of authors associated with the source. When you are writing your literature review, consistency is your best defence against formatting penalties.

Follow this quick reference guide to ensure your APA 7 in-text citation guide is accurate:

  • One author: Use the author’s surname and the year (e.g., Smith, 2024).
  • Two authors: Include both surnames in every citation (e.g., Smith & Jones, 2024). Note the use of the ampersand in parenthetical citations.
  • Three or more authors: Use the first author’s surname followed by "et al." from the very first mention (e.g., Smith et al., 2024).

Many students erroneously use "et al." only after the second mention, a remnant of older style guides. Modern APA 7 rules dictate that for three or more authors, you truncate the citation immediately. How to handle group authors and missing information?

Sometimes, your source may not have an individual author, such as a government report or a corporate white paper. These are classified as group authors. If the group name is long, you may write it out in full the first time and provide an abbreviation in brackets if it appears frequently thereafter.

When you encounter tricky sources, you might also find APA 7th edition: fixing citation loops for complex digital archives helpful. Here are a few tips for managing non-standard author fields:

  • If no author is listed, use the title of the work, italicised, followed by the year.
  • For government documents, use the full name of the department (e.g., Department of Education, 2025).
  • If the author is "Anonymous," use that exact word in place of a name.

How to prevent common multi-author citation errors?

The most frequent errors usually occur due to a lack of attention to detail during the editing phase. Students often confuse parenthetical citations with narrative citations. A narrative citation integrates the author into the sentence structure, while a parenthetical citation places the information in brackets at the end of the claim.

To avoid fixing citation errors repeatedly, keep these distinctions in mind:

  1. Narrative: According to Smith and Jones (2024), the findings suggest...
  2. Parenthetical: The findings suggest a clear correlation (Smith & Jones, 2024).
  3. The Ampersand Rule: Use the ampersand (&) inside parentheses, but write out "and" in the narrative flow.

Consistency is key. If you are inconsistent, your marker may infer a lack of attention to detail, which can affect the perception of your overall thesis quality.
